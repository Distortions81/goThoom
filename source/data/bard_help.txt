# Start here: hear a tune
Open Bard Tools from the Tools menu. This guide is available offline; use Topic above to jump to a task.

1. Select Three Lanterns in the song list. The filled radio button and Selected song line show which tune the controls will use.
2. Choose Preview All. You should hear bass immediately, harp after two seconds, and flute after four. All three parts finish at about 48 seconds.
3. Choose Stop Preview to end it early.
4. Choose Preview Part… and select Water - arpeggios to hear just the harp. This does not change which part you will perform in game.

A preview is heard only by you. You do not need to carry the instruments. Play in Game performs for nearby players and requires the instrument. The topics below walk through creating a tune, performing alone, and setting up a group.

# Create your first tune
1. Choose New. Enter Practice in Tune name, then choose Create & Edit.
2. Replace the editor's starter text with these three lines:

<@title: Practice>
<@instrument: Pine Flute>
@120 c4 d4 e4 g4 g4 e4 d4 c4

3. Choose Check. With no partners entered, this example should say Check passed.
4. Choose Preview in the editor. You should hear eight evenly spaced notes lasting four seconds in total. You can preview again after changing the text, without saving.
5. Choose Save. The main Bard window now uses this version for preview and performance.

Try changing @120 to @60: the same notes now take eight seconds. Change a c4 to p4 to replace that note with silence of the same length. Choose Check and Preview after each edit.

New saves the file as Practice.gttune in Tunes/ in your user data folder. All your characters share this library. The title line controls the displayed song name; changing that line does not rename the file.

# Preview and edit an existing tune
Select the song, then choose Edit. The editor supports search, undo/redo, and the usual copy/paste shortcuts: Ctrl on Windows/Linux, Command on macOS. Text size and colors follow the Text tab in Settings.

Word wrap in the editor toolbar fits long lines to the window and starts enabled. It does not add line breaks to your tune. Turn it off to scroll long lines horizontally; the choice is remembered for all file editors.

Use the editor's Preview to hear the current draft, including unsaved changes. It plays all parts together. To audition a particular part, Save first, then use Preview Part… in the main Bard window and choose the part by name and instrument. The picker also shows its duration.

The main window's Preview plays a saved solo tune; Preview All plays every saved part together. Preview Part… does not change Your part. The Listen row groups local preview controls; the Perform row controls in-game playback. Stop Preview ends local sound; it does not stop a performance already sent to the game.

The selected SoundFont affects the instrument sounds in local playback. Switching character tabs stops your preview.

Check tests every part's notation, instrument restrictions, and command length. It includes the selected character's Play with names, so set up partners before checking a nearly full tune. With Play with empty, it checks solo playback. A warning about different ending times does not fail Check.

After a successful Check, Save before using Play in Game. Check does not equip an instrument or send music. The main Bard controls use the saved file, not the unsaved editor text.

# Perform alone and use an instrument case
1. Select a saved song. For a file with several parts, choose Your part in the main window. To play alone, open Duet / Trio…, clear Play with, then Close that window.
2. In the main Bard window, check Instrument. For the Practice example, select Pine Flute.
3. Choose Play in Game. Review the character, song, part, instrument, and partner list in the confirmation, then confirm.
4. The client retrieves the instrument if needed, equips it, waits for the game's equipment confirmation, and sends the music. The instrument stays equipped afterward.

The Instrument picker immediately saves the selected part's instrument in the song shared by all your characters. Status confirms the saved instrument and part. For a multi-part tune, choose Your part before changing Instrument. Save or close an unsaved editor draft first; the picker cannot overwrite that draft.

An inventory label means the character carries that instrument. A try case label means the character carries an instrument case and the client can request the instrument; it does not mean the case is known to contain it. Without the instrument or a case, that choice is for local preview only.

If your inventory is full during retrieval, the client first stores one carried instrument in the case, preferring an unequipped one. Watch Status and the game's response if retrieval fails.

Stop Playing cancels this character's pending music and sends the game's stop command. It also works while waiting for partners. More… → Put All Instruments Away stops this character and stores all carried instruments in the case, one at a time, restoring the previous left-hand item after successful case use. Stop Playing cancels remaining transfers; items already stored stay stored.

# Set up a duet or trio
Each performer sends their own music to the game. Your part chooses what this character plays. The partner part pickers choose what to SEND to others; they do not start those players or remotely select their instruments.

Example: Blue, Mossy Boots, and Pixy will perform Three Lanterns. Give each performer the following setup in Duet / Trio:

Blue:
Your part: Water - arpeggios (Starbuck Harp)
Play with: Mossy Boots, Pixy

Mossy Boots:
Your part: Dock - bass (Gutbucket Bass)
Play with: Blue, Pixy

Pixy:
Your part: Lantern - melody (Pine Flute)
Play with: Blue, Mossy Boots

1. Each player selects Three Lanterns, opens Duet / Trio…, and enters their setup above. If a player only has their received part, select that tune instead.
2. Check the part durations and assignment warnings. This arrangement should show about 48 seconds for each part.
3. Close Duet / Trio to return to the main Bard window. Each player chooses Play in Game and confirms their own performance.
4. Players who submit first wait for the others. The game starts the group when everyone has submitted. Nobody has to press the button at the same instant.

For a duet, use just two performers: Blue lists Mossy Boots, and Mossy Boots lists Blue. Each selects the intended part. Leave Play with empty to perform alone, including when practicing one voice from an arrangement.

Choose players… lists visible players nearest first. Select one or two and choose OK to fill Play with. For typed names, use commas between players; press Tab to accept a gray name suggestion. Use full names for sharing and receiving. Unique prefixes work for playback, but do not authorize receiving.

Sending parts is optional when everyone already has the music. See Send and receive parts when someone needs a copy. The game supports up to three performers in a synchronized group; files and local previews may contain more parts.

# Send and receive parts
Example: Blue has Three Lanterns and wants to send the bass to Mossy Boots and the melody to Pixy. All three players must equip working sunstones for the private messages.

1. Before sending, Mossy Boots opens Duet / Trio, enters Blue, Pixy in Play with, and checks Receive parts from partners. Pixy does the same with Blue, Mossy Boots.
2. Blue selects Three Lanterns and opens Duet / Trio. Set Your part to Water - arpeggios and Play with to Mossy Boots, Pixy.
3. Beside Mossy Boots, choose Dock - bass. Beside Pixy, choose Lantern - melody. Choose Send Parts.
4. Each recipient waits for the complete part. goThoom saves it as a new tune and selects it for that recipient, preserving its song name, instrument, and music.
5. Recipients can Preview their received tune, then choose Play in Game when the group is ready. Receiving does not equip an instrument or begin playback.

Don't send skips that partner. You can use it to resend to just one person. Cancel Sending cancels messages that have not yet left your character; sent messages cannot be recalled. A completed send does not confirm delivery, so have recipients check that their tune appeared.

Receive parts from partners starts on the first time you open Duet / Trio. Only private thoughts from full names in that session's Play with list are accepted. Blocked and ignored players are excluded. Closing Duet / Trio or switching tabs leaves receiving on. Reconnecting the character or closing the main Bard window turns it off; reopen Duet / Trio and check it again when needed.

Transfers expire after two minutes if incomplete. A part is limited to 8 KiB and 64 messages, with one incomplete transfer per partner. After 16 parts have been saved, the next attempted receipt pauses receiving; enable it again when ready. Received tunes are new files, so existing songs and editor drafts are preserved.

# Share with someone using another client
Send Parts messages contain ordinary Clan Lord notation, with a comment at the start identifying the segment, song, and instrument. A recipient can assemble them manually without goThoom.

1. Copy only each message BODY, beginning with its <...> comment. Leave out the chat timestamp, sender name, and "thinks to you" text.
2. Paste the bodies in numbered order: 1 of 3, then 2 of 3, then 3 of 3. Include every segment exactly once. Keep the notes, rests, punctuation, and backslashes as received.
3. The comments can stay in the music. Set the instrument to the name in the comments, then use that client's normal music or macro workflow.

For automatic receipt in goThoom, use the private sunstone messages themselves. Pasting a transcript into chat does not deliver a part. If Status reports an incomplete or damaged part, ask the sender to send the whole part again; do not try to guess missing notes.

Our <@part: name> markers are comments to other clients. To move a multi-part tune manually, copy one performer's music at a time into separate tunes, including its instrument choice. Copying the entire arrangement as one music stream would play the voices one after another instead of together.

# Understand durations, warnings, and status
Read the Status bar in the main Bard window or Duet / Trio. Hover over a long status message to read it in full.

In Duet / Trio, each part's duration includes its rests and tempo changes. All parts begin from the same time origin. To delay an entrance, put rests at the start of that part; do not wait before submitting Play in Game.

At @120, p8 p8 is two seconds of silence. For example, a four-second part can wait two seconds and then play four quarter notes:
@120 p8 p8 c4 d4 e4 g4

Each part defaults to 120 BPM independently. Put @90 at the start of EVERY part if the whole arrangement should use 90 BPM. A tempo change in the flute part does not change the harp or bass.

"Parts end ... seconds apart" means their calculated ending times differ. If that is unintentional, compare starting rests, repeated phrases, tempo changes, and final rests in Edit. A shorter part can end early deliberately; the warning does not block playback.

"... assigned to both ..." means Your part and the outgoing assignments reuse the same named part. To correct it, choose a different partner part. To have two players double a line intentionally, keep the assignment. The warning describes your setup; it cannot inspect what another player has actually selected.

Preparing the instrument: the client is retrieving or equipping it.
Sending the selected part: music commands are still going to the game.
Waiting for the ensemble: your music has been sent, but the client has not observed your ensemble start.
Playing with the ensemble: the client observed your music start.
Finished: your received part's duration has elapsed, including trailing rests. Other parts may end at different times.

For an ensemble, waiting is not a countdown. If it persists, check everyone's Play with list and that everyone confirmed Play in Game. To retry, have each player Stop Playing, correct the setup, and submit again. Status tracking works even when your local music is muted.

# Notes, rests, tempo, and repeats
Write notation directly in the tune editor. Spaces and line breaks separate phrases visually without adding silence. Use rests for silence.

Note lengths:
c or c2: an eighth note.
C or c4: a quarter note.
c1: a sixteenth note.
c6: a dotted quarter note.
c8: a half note.
p4: a quarter-note rest. Use lowercase p.
At @120, c4 lasts half a second; c8 lasts one second. Length digits run from 1 through 9, so a whole-bar rest in 4/4 is p8 p8, not p16.

Pitches and octaves:
c# is C sharp; b. is B flat.
/c selects high C; following notes stay high until another octave instruction.
\c selects low C; =c returns to middle C for the instrument.
For example, =c /c =c \c =c plays middle, high, middle, low, middle.

Tempo:
@90 sets 90 beats per minute. The range is 60 through 180; the default is 120.
@120 c4 @60 c4 plays a half-second C, then a one-second C.

Repeats:
(c4 d4)2 plays c4 d4 c4 d4.
(=c4 /c4)2 resets to the middle octave at each repeat.
(cde|1f|2g)2 plays cdefcdeg, with a different ending the second time.

For the complete Clan Lord notation reference, including relative tempo, volume, and advanced loops, visit:
https://www.donaldsonworkshop.com/baraboo/cltf.html

# Chords and volume
A chord needs time supplied by the melody or a rest after it. On Starbuck Harp, try this complete tune:

<@title: Chord practice>
<@instrument: Starbuck Harp>
@120 [ceg]4 p4 [dfa]4 p4

You should hear two half-second chords. [ceg]4 describes the first chord; p4 lets it sound while the melody rests. A bare [ceg] at the end of a tune has no following melody time. Use Check to catch missing time or instrument restrictions. Pine Flute cannot play this chord example; use a chord-capable instrument.

For a melody over a chord, try [ceg]4 /c4 =. The high C sounds while the middle C-E-G chord plays; = restores the middle octave afterward. The chord and melody share that half-second rather than taking turns.

%8 sets melody volume to 8 out of 10. Put the volume inside a chord to set the chord line separately: [%6ceg]4 p4. The volume setting continues until changed; %0 silences that line. Changing the Audio mixer's music volume affects what you hear locally, not the volume written into the song.

# Write an arrangement and song details
Start with New, then replace the starter text with the example below. Choose Check, Preview, and Save. Both parts last four seconds at 120 BPM; the bass enters after two seconds.

<@title: Two Voices>
<@composer: Your name>
<@tags: duet, practice>
<@part: Melody>
<@instrument: Pine Flute>
@120 c4 d4 e4 g4 g4 e4 d4 c4
<@part: Bass>
<@instrument: Gutbucket Bass>
@120 p8 p8 c8 g8

<@title: ...> supplies the library title; otherwise the filename is used.
<@composer: ...> supplies the composer name.
<@tags: duet, practice> adds two searchable tags.
<@part: Melody> starts a named performer's music. Names must be nonempty and unique in the file.
<@instrument: Pine Flute> sets that part's instrument.

Keep title, composer, and tags before the first part. Each part continues until the next <@part: ...> line or the end of the file. Each starts at time zero, with its own tempo and rests. Without any part markers, the file is a solo tune. An instrument line before the first part supplies the default for parts without their own instrument.

Add explanatory comments such as <Wait for the bass.> or <Bar 4>. Comments may span lines or contain nested comments. They are not sent to the game. Put metadata on separate lines and spell its keys as shown; Check reports unknown keys.

In metadata values, use &lt; for <, &gt; for >, and &amp; for &. Older ; comments and ;@name: value metadata work too; a semicolon comment ends at the end of its line. Without instrument metadata or a saved legacy preference, the instrument defaults to Lucky Lyra.

# Import songs and manage the library
Import accepts .gttune, .tune, and .txt files containing UTF-8 Clan Lord notation, up to 256 KiB. It saves a new .gttune copy in Tunes/ and opens that copy for editing, keeping the song details, parts, and instruments. Choose Check, fix any reported problem, then Save. The original file stays where it was. If the destination .gttune filename already exists, rename the incoming file before importing.

To use music copied from a website or another tool, choose New and paste only its notation into the editor. For example, a classic command:
/use /tempo 160 c4 d4 e4 g4
becomes this tune text:
@160 c4 d4 e4 g4

For music spread over several /use commands, join that performer's note text in order. Remove command options such as /part and /with names, but keep musical octave marks such as /c and \c. Preserve any tempo changes. Do not paste macro triggers, quoted command wrappers, or several alternative exports of the same song. Use Check afterward.

MIDI must first be converted to Clan Lord notation in a conversion tool. A .tune filename alone does not guarantee its contents are notation: for a tool's own project format, copy or export the music text. If an older text file is rejected for encoding, save a UTF-8 copy in a text editor before importing.

More… → Open Folder shows Tunes/ on disk. Files there are shared by all characters. Existing .tune and .txt files remain editable in place; you do not need to rename them. New songs and received parts default to .gttune. Use More… → Refresh after adding, renaming, or changing files outside goThoom. Use the titlebar search for titles, composers, tags, part names, or instruments; Tag narrows the list to one tag, and Songs - sort by changes its order.

Select a song and choose More… → Delete Song… to permanently delete its file and instrument preference after confirmation, including any open draft. Copy files out with Open Folder if you want a backup first. Three Lanterns is installed once; editing it preserves your copy, and deleting it keeps it from being re-added.

A performance uses at most five music commands per part, including partner names. This is separate from the number of performers and from the number of sunstone messages used for sharing. If Check says a part is too long, replace repeated music with loops, for example (c4 d4)4 instead of writing that pair four times. Shortening comments will not help: they are already omitted from the game commands.

# Character tabs and stopping
The main Bard window follows the selected character tab. Each session keeps its own selected song, Your part, Play with list, and receiving choice while Bard Tools remains open. The tune files themselves are shared.

To play with your own characters, configure and confirm Play in Game for the first character, switch tabs, then configure and confirm the next. Each must list the other performers, just as separate players would. Switching tabs leaves submitted performances, instrument transfers, and outgoing part messages running on their original character. It stops local preview.

Stop Preview: stops only local audition sound.
Stop Playing: stops the selected character's performance or waiting state and cancels that character's remaining instrument transfers.
Cancel Sending: cancels the selected character's unsent part messages.

Closing Duet / Trio keeps that session's receiving choice active. Closing the main Bard window stops performances started through Bard Tools for ALL characters, cancels their remaining transfers and unsent messages, and disables receiving. Already saved tunes and completed transfers remain. Closing Help only closes this guide.

# Troubleshoot a specific problem
Preview is silent:
Check that music is enabled and audible in the Audio mixer. Then use Check in the editor. For a chord, make sure a note or rest follows it; [ceg]4 p4 gives a chord time to sound. If only the opening is silent, inspect starting rests: Three Lanterns' flute intentionally waits four seconds.

Preview sounds like the old version:
The main window reads the saved file. Save the editor draft, or use Preview inside the editor to hear unsaved changes. A changed Instrument choice also needs a new preview to hear it.

Play in Game is disabled:
Hover over it for the reason. Select a valid tune, connect the character, and carry its instrument or an instrument case. A character watching a movie cannot perform in game.

The instrument picker is disabled:
Save or close the unsaved editor draft, then change Instrument. For an arrangement, select Your part beside Part instrument in the main window first so you change the intended performer.

Retrieving or storing an instrument failed:
Read Status and the game's response. A try case label does not prove the instrument is in the case. If inventory is full and no carried instrument can be stored to make room, free a slot and retry.

The group stays waiting:
Check each player's Play with list: everyone names the others, not themselves. Check that every player confirmed Play in Game and successfully equipped the instrument. Sending a part or choosing a preview does not submit a performance. Have everyone Stop Playing before correcting the setup and retrying.

No part arrived, or it was rejected as incomplete or damaged:
On the recipient's character, open Duet / Trio, enter the sender's FULL name in Play with, and enable Receive parts from partners. Check both players' working sunstones and that the sender is not blocked or ignored. Then resend the entire part. Reconnects, closing Bard, and the receiving limit can turn receiving off; unfinished transfers expire after two minutes.

Send Parts says the song or connection changed:
Close and reopen Duet / Trio after saving the song, selecting another song, or reconnecting. Review the assignments before sending again. If a playback confirmation is out of date, reopen Play in Game to review the current character and song.
